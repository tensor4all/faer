# LQ factorization and null spaces

tenferro-rs#2423 step 1 / tensor4all/faer#130 PR 1.

## Decisions

- **LQ is the QR factorization of the transposed view.** `lq_in_place` calls the existing blocked
  QR kernel on `A.transpose_mut()`, and `copy_l`/`unitary`/`reconstruct`/`null` read the result
  with LQ's conventions. No second blocked factorizer exists to keep in step with the QR one, and
  the arithmetic is QR's, so the numerical behaviour (rank estimate, block sizes, team schedule)
  is QR's as well.
- **The packed QR kernel gained a `pub(crate)` entry.** `qr_in_place` was split into the
  layout-dispatch wrapper plus `qr_in_place_packed`, which is today's body. The row-major
  adaptation copies into a heap buffer, and an LQ input presents a row-major layout to the
  transposed view on every call, so going through the public entry would allocate per call for
  the whole class. The cost of not copying is the strided panel walk that every LQ pays,
  including LAPACK's `?gelqf`; the trailing block update is a matmul and packs both operands.
- **The gauge is applied at unpack time, not inside the factorization.** `copy_l`,
  `copy_r`, `unitary` and `null` take `positive: bool`; the packed form stays raw. The consumers
  that own the policy (`tenferro-linalg`'s `QrGauge`, `tlinalg`'s documented "raw faer factors")
  keep it, and the factor extraction is the only place where the compensating phases are cheap.
- **`unitary` and `null` are one start-block routine.** `Q` and its complement are rows
  `offset..offset+q` of the full QR basis applied to an identity block; `null` is the same call
  with `offset = k`. Two public wrappers, one implementation, and the compact `Q` is the full
  `Q`'s leading block, which the tests assert.
- Output conventions (compact `L` `m x k` and `Q` `k x n`; full `L` `m x n` and `Q` `n x n`; the
  null block being the trailing part of the full `Q`; the real-nonnegative triangular diagonal)
  follow MatrixAlgebraKit.jl, so a Julia front end can map them one to one. `k` is `min(m, n)`, not
  the reported rank: on rank-deficient input the null block is the complement of the column (or
  row) space that the factorization actually determines, exactly as the reference does.

## Verification conclusions and constraints

- The LAPACK family makes 576 sequential calls and an 18-call parallel group per dtype
  (f64/f32/c64/c32): three shapes x two passes at `Par::Seq`, on a 2-thread team and on a
  4-thread team, covering
  empty shapes, rank-deficient fixtures (exact zero rows), `m < n` and `m > n`, eight fixture
  kinds including a zero matrix, a repeated-diagonal and two extreme-scale cases, four layouts
  (transposed and reversed strides), block sizes 1 and 4, and reused output buffers. Reconstruction,
  orthonormality of the compact and full `Q`, the null block continuing the full `Q`, `A N^H = 0`,
  the positive gauge, and unchanged factors after the extraction all pass. The rank LQ reports
  is the QR kernel's estimate for the transposed view, an upper bound that a run of zero rows
  inside the block structure can leave above the exact rank; the reference behaves the same way
  and the tests assert the bound, not equality.
- Look-ahead: for LQ the hidden panel is a block of *rows* of `A`, which the transposed view
  presents with `A.col_stride()` as its column stride. Two `#[cfg(test)]`-forced cases
  (`LOOKAHEAD_FORCE`) check that the step actually runs at 4 and 8 participants for wide and
  square inputs, that the packed factor matches the sequential one to rounding, that `L Q = A`
  and `Q`'s rows stay orthonormal, and that a rank loss inside a prefactored panel keeps the
  same rank.
- Cross-check against LAPACK's own `?gelqf` and `?orglq`/`?unglq` on shared fixtures, with both
  sides gauged so the factors are unique: largest normwise ratio 0.44 for `Q` and 0.22 for `L`
  against the 30 threshold, all four types. This is opt-in (`--cfg openblas` plus `#[ignore]`)
  because it needs a linked LAPACK, which no other test in this repository does.
- The serial LQ pipeline allocates nothing (`faer/tests/lq_no_alloc.rs`, eight shapes). Two
  inherited limits are not covered by that claim and are not new: the block-coefficient upgrade
  allocates its partials when the `V^H V` reduction is split across threads, and the matmul
  fallback on hosts without AVX2/FMA allocates (PATCHSET rows #58/#56).
- OpenBLAS timing (`dgelqf` + `dorglq`, one core and eight threads, paired) belongs to the
  faer-benchmark campaign; nothing here is timed and no speed claim is made.

## Speed against OpenBLAS `?gelqf` + `?orglq` (diagnostic, not a campaign page)

The campaign has no LQ suite, so this is a standalone driver
(`faer-benchmark/probes/lq-vs-lapack`, untracked there): both arms in one process, interleaved,
`?gelqf` + `?orglq`/`?zunglq` on the LAPACK side and `lq_in_place` + `copy_l` + `unitary` on the
faer side, so both produce the same three outputs and the same work.

Measurement identities. Host `broccoli`, AMD EPYC 7702P, 1 NUMA node, SMT off, boost off,
governor performance, 16 MiB L3 per 4-core CCX, following the campaign's `zen2-cpu` profile thread
rows: 1 thread on CPU 0, 8 threads on CPUs 0-7 (one CCD, two L3 domains, which that profile declares
for its 8T row). Observed: faer `2cbeceb`, OpenBLAS `0.3.8` (`config=OpenBLAS 0.3.8 NO_LAPACKE
DYNAMIC_ARCH NO_AFFINITY Zen MAX_THREADS=64 parallel=1 core=Zen`, the distribution's pthread build,
not the campaign arm's 0.3.32). 21 samples per cell at 1T (three sessions, seven repetitions) and
35 at 8T (five sessions), per-cell statistic the **minimum**, because the medians of several
sessions were contaminated: the measured CPUs carried 8-30% foreign load throughout
(`busy_before` 0.07-0.38 across all cells) and one 8T session at host load 30 produced 136 ms
medians against a 2.6 ms minimum for the same cell. Those sessions are reported, not hidden, and
their numbers are excluded by taking the minimum rather than by re-running until they looked
good. No cell passed the campaign's 5% idle gate, so none of this is a campaign claim.

What the cells say. At one thread the two implementations are within 20% of each other over
128×128-multiple shapes (faer 0.82-1.44 of OpenBLAS), and the ordering follows the shape: faer is
faster for complex input and for a wide `A` (a tall transposed panel), OpenBLAS is faster for a tall
`A` and for the 1024 square. At eight threads faer is faster in seven of eight cells and by 4-9×
where OpenBLAS's threaded LAPACK overhead dominates a small problem; at the 1024 square the two are
level (0.98). LQ's parallel speed-up is modest (1.08-2.48×) because only the trailing update is
GEMM-shaped while the panel walks a row of `A` with stride `A.col_stride()`; that is the price of
the transposed-view kernel, and it is the number a native LQ panel would have to beat. What was
not measured: the campaign's own OpenBLAS 0.3.32 arm, an idle-gated window, the 4T row, and shapes
above 1024.
